# Afterworld — Acts II and III

Act I is the story so far: you wake at the Waystone Camp, free the Three Who Wait, and fall the five
Wardens, and "the world, at last, lets out its breath." This document carries the story on through
the Nether and the End. It builds on what Act I already says:

- **Edicts** are old rules written into stone and storm.
- **The Bell-Ringer** takes memory with every toll.
- **The King of Seconds** keeps time and refuses the seconds he dislikes.
- **The Unremembered village's census** lists people who were never born.
- **The Morrow Court** remembers the dead "so that they may rest".
- **Torn Chronicle pages** can be restored.

## The turn at the end of Act I

When Isolde's epilogue ends, the waystone on the dais flickers. In the plaza stands a second
waystone that **was never built**. Nobody remembers raising it, yet it is old and worn as if it had
always been there. Its name plate is blank.

Breathing out let go of everything the world had been holding in. The Bell-Ringer's stolen memories
did not vanish; they sank. The seconds the King refused did not vanish either; they drifted up. The
blank waystone is the first of the forgotten things coming home.

Touching it starts Act II. Until then, the camp's keepers each have a line about it, which tells the
player the story goes on.

## Act II — The Unremembered (the Nether)

The Nether becomes **the Unremembered**: where everything the world forgot ends up. It holds places
nobody remembers building, the people in the census who were never born, and names that were
struck out. It is warm the way old embers are: red light, ash and quiet.

### Lands (custom Nether biomes)

| Land | Replaces | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| The Ashen Atlas | Nether wastes | A city of forgotten places, streets that lead into each other. The Act's hub. |
| The Hall of Unsaid Words | Basalt deltas | Basalt columns carved with sentences nobody finished. |
| The Garden of Old Promises | Crimson forest | Every vow ever broken, grown as red trees. |
| The Shore of Last Sight | Soul sand valley | Where faces go when the last person who knew them dies. |
| The Census Fields | Warped forest | The never-born, waiting in blue light for names. |

### People

- **Halvard the Census Clerk:** a ghost from the Unremembered village who never stopped counting.
  He is the Act's guide and keeper, and the Ashen Atlas's waystone keeper.
- **Sister Ines:** she follows you down. The Morrow Court's work suddenly makes sense to her, and
  frightens her.
- **The Never-Born:** quiet figures who ask you to name them. Naming one (a short choice) gives it a
  life of a kind and a small boon. Leaving them nameless is also a choice, and the story notices.

### Chapters

- **Chapter IV — The Forgotten Road.**
  - Through the blank waystone to the Ashen Atlas.
  - Find Halvard.
  - Restore three lost names, one each from the Hall, the Garden and the Shore. Each is a short
    puzzle site with a guardian.
  - The names spell out the reason the Bell rang: it was set to make the world forget **who wrote
    the Edicts**.
- **Chapter V — The Bell Below.**
  - The Bell-Ringer's true bell hangs in the Unremembered and is still ringing.
  - Here it takes the player's own memories: Chronicle pages tear while you fight near it. The
    restore mechanic from Act I becomes part of the fight.
  - Boss: **the Hollow Toll**, the Bell's echo given a body. Phases shift between memories of the
    Act I bosses.
- **Chapter VI — The Struck Name.**
  - At the Atlas's heart stands the palace of the first person the world forgot: **the Scribe**,
    who wrote the Edicts and was erased so the rules would seem to have "always been so".
  - Boss: **the Ash Regent**, the Scribe's forgotten guard. He is a mirror of the Gilded Warden
    (Act I's last Warden): a judge with no one left to judge.
  - Ending: the Scribe's name is spoken aloud, and an End portal in the Ashen Atlas lights (or the
    vanilla portal's frame, if it was found). The Scribe's name opens the Archive.

## Act III — The Archive of Almost (the End)

The End becomes **the Archive of Almost**: everything that almost happened. It holds every second the
King of Seconds refused and every choice that went the other way. The islands are its shelves. Past
the outer islands lies **Upside Country**: ruins of worlds that almost were, hanging upside down
over nothing.

### Lands

- **The Reading Islands:** the central islands, quiet and lit like a library at night. This is the
  Archivist's hall.
- **Upside Country:** the outer islands turned over. Gravity is the right way up near their
  undersides. It is traversal-heavy: the glide, the double jump and the Wayfarer's Hook all pay off
  here.
- **The Margins:** the edge of the Archive, where pages stop being written. Here the Unwritten is
  strongest.

### People

- **The Archivist:** an Enderman scholar, tall and silent, who speaks only by holding up pages. He
  shelves what almost happened and is terrified of the Margins.
- **Your Almosts:** echoes of you who made other choices: another origin, another discipline,
  another Edict. Some help and some fight. They are built from the player's own garb and history,
  so every player meets different ones.

### Chapters

- **Chapter VII — The Archive.**
  - Arrive by the Scribe's name.
  - Help the Archivist re-shelve three almost-histories. Each is a small replay of an Act I moment
    that ends differently: the Pilgrim who arrived, the King who kept every second, the camp that
    was never built.
- **Chapter VIII — Upside Country.**
  - Cross the inverted ruins to the three Marginal Spires. Each is a traversal challenge ending in a
    duel with one of your Almosts.
- **Chapter IX — The Unwritten.**
  - The antagonist since Act I. The anomalies, the "loose Edicts", were its bites.
  - The Unwritten is the blank space between rules. It wants a world with no rules at all: perfect
    freedom, in which nothing holds together.
  - Final boss in the Margins. The arena unwrites itself: blocks vanish, then return as you strike
    Edicts back into place.

### The ending: three choices

At the end the Scribe's pen lies in reach.

| Choice | What happens | After |
|---|---|---|
| **Restore** | Rewrite the old Edicts as they were. | The world settles. The Wardens return as kind guardians, and the Unremembered and the Archive close. Calm, safe and fixed. |
| **Contain** | Bind the Unwritten into the waystones. | The world keeps moving, freedom and danger in balance. Isolde becomes keeper of the seal, and the waystones hum a new note. |
| **Exploit** | Take the pen and write your own Edict. | You become the new Scribe. Unlocks "New Story+", where you choose world rules (for example longer nights, no fall damage, or loose Edicts everywhere). An uneasy ending: Isolde no longer quite recognises you. |

Each choice has its own epilogue, with the narrator and the keepers. Factions shift: the Crown
favours Restore, the Bureau Exploit, and the Guild and the Court Contain.
